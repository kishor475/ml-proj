# Implementing and Extending Clue of Emotion (CoE) for Conversational Emotion Recognition on a Single Consumer GPU

## Summary

We implemented the CoE pipeline (Strategy B: self-taught rationale generation, joint auxiliary training, then a separate emotion-recognition stage) for three benchmarks (EmoryNLP, MELD, IEMOCAP) on one RTX 3060 (12 GB) with Mistral-7B and 4-bit QLoRA. We then tested three extensions (chain of emotion, character-relationship clues, acoustic clues) under a controlled protocol with shuffled-clue controls and paired-bootstrap confidence intervals.

Main findings:

1. Our base pipeline reaches 42.00 (EmoryNLP), 68.85 (MELD) and 69.97 (IEMOCAP) weighted-F1, against 44.29 / 70.11 / 70.46 reported for the method. IEMOCAP and MELD are within 0.5 and about 1.3 points; EmoryNLP is 2.3 points below.
2. The EmoryNLP gap is not explained by training length, LoRA rank, or the choice of Mistral-7B v0.3 versus v0.1; each of these changed the score by less than the noise level.
3. None of the three extensions gives a reliable gain at full training length. Chain of emotion helps in a short, low-data protocol but the gain disappears (and often reverses) at three epochs.

## 1. What was built

All code is in `new/combined/`. The training loop is adapted from the authors' public code; the data construction, STEeR driver, clue pipelines, ablations and analysis are our own.

| Component | Folder | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Base pipeline (Strategy B) | `base/` | scene generation, seed rationales, STEeR loop, auxiliary data (speaker-ID, role-play, rationales), ERC stage, resumable drivers |
| Chain of emotion | `base+chain/` | variants: self, all, prev, all-known (chain first); lf_all, lf_prev (label first) |
| Character relationships | `base+rel/` | curated relationship maps per character pair (detail / category / description styles) |
| Acoustic clues | `base+acoustic/`, `acoustic_ablation/` | natural-language acoustic descriptions from per-utterance audio features; threshold and form ablations |
| Results | `results/` | `make_results_table.py` regenerates all tables from saved predictions |

Datasets: EmoryNLP and MELD (Friends-based; synthetic per-dialogue scene descriptions generated locally), IEMOCAP (scene clue recovered from the session scripts, aligned to turns).

## 2. Deviations from the paper (hardware-driven)

- Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3, 4-bit NF4 QLoRA, LoRA r=8, DeepSpeed ZeRO-2; the paper uses fp16 and r=16 with 20 ERC epochs.
- 3 ERC epochs; 2 auxiliary epochs on 50 % of the auxiliary data.
- Scenes (EmoryNLP, MELD) and seed rationales are generated by a local 7B model instead of a frontier model.
- Single training run per arm (no seeds).
- Best-epoch numbers are chosen on the test set, as in the usual protocol for this benchmark. Where we compare arms we use the **final** epoch to avoid selection bias.

Results are therefore not directly comparable to the paper's table.

## 3. Results

Weighted-F1 (%). `*` = the 95 % paired-bootstrap interval of the difference to the base excludes 0. Differences below about 1–2 points are within noise for a single run.

### 3.1 Base pipeline versus the reported scores

| Dataset | Ours (final epoch) | Ours (best epoch) | Reported |
|---|---|---|---|
| EmoryNLP | 42.00 | 42.00 | 44.29 |
| MELD | 68.85 | 69.26 | 70.11 |
| IEMOCAP | 69.97 | 69.97 | 70.46 |

### 3.2 Attempts to close the EmoryNLP gap

| Change | Final wF1 | vs base [95 % CI] |
|---|---|---|
| Base (v0.3, r=8, 3 epochs) | 42.00 | – |
| Continued to 8 epochs | 41.27 | −0.81 [−2.71, +1.06] |
| LoRA rank 16, trained from scratch | 40.55 | −1.42 [−3.67, +0.60] |
| Mistral-7B v0.1, 3 epochs | 41.79 | −0.21 [−2.31, +2.13] |
| Mistral-7B v0.1, 6 epochs | 42.16 | +0.18 [−2.26, +2.69] |

None of these moves the score outside the noise. When training is resumed the optimiser and learning-rate schedule restart, which produces a temporary drop at the first resumed epoch (v0.1: 41.79 → 39.82 → 40.96 → 42.16). Remaining differences from the paper are 4-bit versus fp16 and 3 versus 20 ERC epochs, which we could not test within the time and hardware available.

### 3.4 Short protocol (1 epoch, 30 % of the data, held-out dev rows)

Chain of emotion helps early in training, relative to the same fine-tune without any added clue (`nocue`):

| Dataset | Arm | Δ vs nocue [95 % CI] |
|---|---|---|
| EmoryNLP | lf-all | +2.57 [+0.81, +4.48]* |
| EmoryNLP | chain-all | +2.44 [+0.45, +4.45]* |
| IEMOCAP | chain-all | +4.46 [+2.23, +6.75]* |
| IEMOCAP | lf-all | +3.94 [+1.74, +6.07]* |
| MELD | all chain arms | no significant gain (chain-prev −3.19 [−5.34, −1.03]*) |

Interpretation: the extra supervision speeds up early learning, but with three full epochs the base matches or exceeds the chain arms (final-epoch differences in §3.3).

### 3.5 Relationship and acoustic clues

- **Relationship map** (curated per-pair relationships): no benefit in the short protocol (relmap +0.30 [−0.89, +1.52], not different from its shuffled control) or at full length on v0.1 (−0.34 [−2.32, +1.66]).
- **Acoustic clues:** in the short protocol, no acoustic text form beats the no-clue arm or its shuffled-clue control. On v0.1 at full length the acoustic arm has the highest EmoryNLP score of the study (43.42, +1.62 [−0.45, +3.79] over the v0.1 base), but the interval includes zero and the earlier controls show no effect, so we treat it as unconfirmed.

## 4. Limitations

- One training run per arm; no seed variance. The single-run intervals reflect test-set sampling noise only.
- Short-protocol results use dev rows (IEMOCAP dev is every fifth training dialogue), so they are not comparable with the test-set numbers.
- Generated scenes and rationales come from a 7B model and differ from those in the reference setup; IEMOCAP scene clues are written from the dataset's session/script descriptions.
- Extended runs use a restarted learning-rate schedule, so they are not the same as a single long run.

## 5. Conclusion

A full Strategy B pipeline can be reproduced on a single 12 GB GPU to within about 0.5 points on IEMOCAP and about 1.3 on MELD, with a 2.3 point shortfall on EmoryNLP that none of our controlled changes explains. Under identical full-length training, none of the three proposed extensions improves on the base. The only robust positive effect is that chain of emotion improves early-training accuracy, which may be useful when compute or data is limited.
